How to Price Products for a Small Business

A good starting price needs to cover the costs of delivering the product and leave room for the work of running the business.

Start with a cost floor

Add the direct product cost and unavoidable per-sale fees. This creates a floor, not necessarily the final customer price.

Choose a target margin

A target margin helps you work backwards from the share of revenue you want to retain. If your base cost is $40 and your target margin is 40%, a simple price estimate is $40 ÷ (1 − .40) = $66.67.

Try the pricing calculator

Check the market and the value

Cost-based pricing is only a starting point. Compare alternatives customers can realistically choose and consider the outcome your product creates. Do not copy a competitor without understanding differences in quality, service, or costs.

Review after real sales

Track refunds, support time, discounts, and actual fees. A price that looks profitable on paper may need revision after real operating data appears.
